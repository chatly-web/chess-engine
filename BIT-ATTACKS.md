# Bit-mask attack-detection prototype

A separate original C++ prototype replaces repeated attacked-square ray walking with precomputed board-geometry masks and nearest-blocker bit scans. Pawn, knight and king geometry is generated from coordinates at startup. Occupied, pawn and knight bitmaps are maintained on all temporary legality/SEE boards as well as real search states. These are rules-geometry tables, not opening books, endgame tablebases, move recommendations or evaluation weights. No existing engine implementation or constants were imported.

The control, with the feature disabled, is byte-identical to the faster build. The candidate passed 640,000 attacked-square comparisons against the rules library across 5,000 legal positions and their colour mirrors, including friendly occupied endpoints. Normal perft/legal/capture/symmetry and protocol checks passed. Across 204 fixed-depth search comparisons, its moves, scores, depths and node counts matched the faster build. Address/undefined-behaviour sanitizers also reconstruct geometry on temporary states; those checks passed. These remain finite checks.

A serial paired benchmark on 16 fresh human prefixes, two passes at depth 6, measured a median baseline/candidate wall-time ratio of **1.0827**, about **8.3% more throughput** (about 7.6% less time for the same work). This fell below the predeclared 1.15 threshold for proceeding to strength matches, so this branch stopped before match collection. It makes no tournament, accuracy or strength claim and changes no recommended executable. No further tuning or extra sample collection was performed for this prototype.

Frozen source, control/candidate executables, build flags, data audit, raw benchmark, attack-map audit, fixed-depth comparison and the explicit stop decision are in `source/cpp/experimental/bit_attacks/` and `bit-attacks-pass/`. The source and weights remain independent of GNU/Stockfish. Python is offline validation; the prototype itself is C++17.
